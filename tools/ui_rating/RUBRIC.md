# Blind rating rubric — frozen November 11, 2026 before the first reading

You are rating screenshots of the user interface of a PC grand-strategy game set in Napoleonic Europe (1805).
The player gives orders to marshals, reads ledgers (forces, provinces, the treasury), conducts diplomacy with the
courts of Europe, answers letters of state, and watches battles resolve. Most frames are 1600x900; one is
5120x1440 (an ultrawide monitor) and one is the same window with the Interface Scale at 2.0.

Rate EACH frame 1–10 for the quality of its UI/UX as a player would experience it, judging:

- R1 Readable — text size and legibility.
- R2 Hierarchy — the one thing the screen is for is the largest or first element; the eye lands on it.
- R3 Contrast — text against its background.
- R4 Consistency — chrome, buttons, headings and spacing match one visual system.
- R5 Affordance — what is clickable looks clickable; what looks clickable is; disabled states are clear.
- R6 Feedback — the screen shows state and the result of actions (as far as a still can show it).
- R7 Fit — nothing clipped, overflowing, crammed, or floating in wasted space.
- R8 Discoverability — the key action is findable without a manual.
- Visual craft — hierarchy and layout, consistency, polish (alignment, spacing, finish), and period feel
  (does it feel like a game about 1805, not a web form or a debug panel?).

Anchors (use the whole scale; half points are not allowed):
- 10 — as good as the best shipped commercial strategy games' UI (Victoria 3, Total War, Crusader Kings III);
       every element purposeful; rich period feel; flawless hierarchy and finish.
- 9  — commercial quality; a reviewer would praise the UI; only small nitpicks.
- 8  — polished; attractive and consistent; a few plain or dense areas.
- 7  — competent and coherent; clearly themed; but text-heavy or plain in places, or with a few crude elements.
- 6  — functional; the theme is present but flat; walls of text; generic widgets.
- 5  — functional but crude; inconsistent or debug-like elements.
- 3–4 — hard to read or use; broken layout.
- 1–2 — broken.

Report, for each frame: `Fnn: <score> — <one line: the main reason, and the single change that would raise it most>`.
Then: `OVERALL: <score 1–10>` for the client as a whole, with two sentences of reasons.
Judge only what you see. Do not guess the game's history or what changed between versions.
