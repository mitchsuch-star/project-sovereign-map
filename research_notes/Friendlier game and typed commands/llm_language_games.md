# LLM free-text language games and mods, 2023 to Nov 2026: reception and lessons

Scope note: about 20 tool calls were made. Many searches returned aggregator summaries and several Steam/SteamDB/GDC pages returned 403 or the storefront homepage. Figures are labelled MEASURED (a store page or database number) or OPINION. Games I could not check are listed under Gaps. Today = Nov 19, 2026.

## 1. Free-text conversation and persuasion games: ratings, review counts, recurring praise and complaints

### Takeaway
The persuasion and detective games built on free text sit mostly at Mixed to Very Positive on Steam, with small review counts. Whispers from the Star (Very Positive) is the only strong rating I verified. Suck Up! and Vaudeville are Mixed. Praise centres on the novelty of a character answering what you actually said. Complaints centre on slowness, AI limits and thin content. Concurrency for Whispers collapsed after launch.

### Cited Findings
- Suck Up! (Proxima, $16.99, released Oct 1, 2025 per Steam). MEASURED: Mixed, 62% positive of 233 reviews; recent 30 days 64% of 14 reviews (Steam page, fetched Nov 2026). Featured reviews praise voice interaction and NPC responses that feel "real" (curated sample, may be unrepresentative) — [Steam: Suck Up!](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2726370/Suck_Up/)
- Suck Up! aggregators: 18 players in-game, average playtime about 2.5 h, no sales estimate; one source says 59% of 266 reviews; release date conflicts between sources (Sept 4 vs Oct 1, 2025). Reviewers praise talking to an AI vampire and criticise a lack of endgame variety (aggregator summaries, lower reliability) — [Datahumble](https://datahumble.com/suck-up-2726370); [Nodal](https://nodal.gg/game/suck-up-2726370)
- Suck Up! was announced in 2024 (a developer forum post describes a four-person team with a trust system for characters) — [OpenAI forum](https://community.openai.com/t/vampire-game-where-you-convince-llm-to-let-you-in/604295)
- Vaudeville (Bumblebee Studios; early access Jun 30, 2023; 1.0 Nov 28, 2025; $19.99). MEASURED: Mixed, 47% positive of 288 reviews on the Steam page I fetched; a search snippet earlier showed Mixed with 269 reviews. Steam lists "dialogues generated with the help of a conversational AI" — [Steam: Vaudeville](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2240920)
- Vaudeville: outlet coverage cites disappointment over poor AI performance, slow load times and limited settings; the developer's Jan 2024 pre-mortem said reception was "very polarised" and sales were about 7,500 then (as reported in a search summary; I could not open the article body) — [Game Developer pre-mortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/vaudeville-a-pre-mortem)
- Whispers from the Star (Anuttacon, Aug 2025). MEASURED: SteamDB showed Very Positive, 84.6% positive of about 1,200 reviews; a June 29, 2026 snapshot shows 82% of 1,618 reviews. All-time peak 964 concurrent on Aug 15, 2025; last-30-day average about 7.35 and June 2026 average 5.53 players (SteamCharts via search summary). Game8 says play is limited to 40-60 minutes per day for free members — [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/3730100/graphs); [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/3730100); [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/3730100/Whispers_from_the_Star); [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/games/whispers-from-the-star)
- Whispers coverage: Notebookcheck said the launch was Very Positive but might divide gamers over heavy AI integration — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-launch-New-interactive-fiction-game-debuts-to-Very-Positive-reviews-may-divide-gamers-over-heavy-AI-integration.1087821.0.html)
- inZOI "Smart Zoi" (Krafton, NVIDIA ACE). Concept praised as novel even by players who hit bugs; main complaints were GPU cost (frame rates under 20 fps reported on high-end cards) and experimental bugs. Players can steer characters by typing goals ("Zoi Pen"). Steam score figures conflict (86% of about 2,700 at launch vs 82% of 10,000+); the base-game criticism was thin content, not the AI — [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/inzoi-is-what-happens-when-the-sims-meets-ai-and-the-results-are-gloriously-unstable); [fan wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/inzoi/wiki/reception-and-reviews)
- Mecha BREAK (NVIDIA ACE, mechanic Martel, voice-driven). Press at the Aug 2024 reveal called it a proof of concept applied to one NPC; no post-launch player sentiment found — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/nvidias-first-ai-npc-debuts-in-mecha-break-minitron-4b-model-only-requires-2gb-of-vram)
- Covert Protocol (Inworld/NVIDIA) was a tech demo, not a shipped game — same Tom's Hardware / [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/nvidia-ace-demo-first-time-in-actual-game-ai-powered-interactions-mecha-break) coverage.
- Death by AI: the one I could verify is a free Jackbox-style party game where an AI judges players' typed survival strategies (itch.io / Kotaku listing, mobile and web). No Steam review data found — [itch.io](https://bobbodev.itch.io/death-by-ai); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/games/death-by-ai)

### Inferences
- The best-received format (Whispers, Suck Up!) pairs one character, one clear goal and a reply that is performed (voice or animation). The weakest-rated (Vaudeville) puts free text on a whodunit with slow or limited AI.
- Whispers' fall from 964 peak to single-digit averages is consistent with novelty decay plus a daily play cap, but that is my inference; no retention figure was published.

### Gaps
- Not verified at all: 1001 Nights, Retail Mage, Dead Meat, AI2U, Verbal Verdict, Uncover the Smoking Gun, Yandere AI Girlfriend Simulator, AI Dungeon (2023-26 figures), Inworld Origins. Searches did not surface them, so I give no numbers.
- No Steam score for Death by AI. No sales figures for Suck Up! or Whispers. Steam review text itself was not readable (store pages return only curated snippets).

## 2. Strategy and role-playing games and mods with typed language

### Takeaway
Mods are alive but niche in evidence: Voices of the Court (CK3), Mantella (Skyrim/Fallout 4) and Bannerlord LLM mods all exist and are maintained into 2026. Commercial strategy titles with typed or spoken orders exist only as small early-access or demo releases, with Mixed to tiny samples of reviews. I found no big-publisher strategy game shipping LLM orders or diplomacy.

### Cited Findings
- Voices of the Court (CK3): LLM conversation with characters, can affect game state to an extent. Version 2.0 "Community Edition" supports up to 16 characters, is open source (GPLv3). Needs an LLM provider (player2 app needs no key). Steam comments mention incompatibility between old and new versions and provider errors. No subscriber count found — [Workshop comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/3122170466); [mirror](https://catalogue.smods.ru/archives/414014)
- Mantella (Skyrim/Fallout 4): speech-to-text, LLM and text-to-speech; claims 2,000+ NPCs; NPCs can act on conversation (follow, attack, forgive); group conversations; a 2026 overhaul added memory of conversations (PC Gamer and a Shazoo item dated Apr 27, 2026). Nexus download counts did not load; about 284 GitHub stars per one aggregator — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/the-elder-scrolls/this-skyrim-mod-that-makes-npcs-ai-chatterboxes-just-got-a-massive-overhaul/); [Nexus](https://www.nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/98631); [GitHub](https://github.com/art-from-the-machine/Mantella)
- Bannerlord: a Feb 2023 mod replaced dialogue trees with free text via ChatGPT; reviewers noted very long load times and robotic dialogue; later mods use Inworld voice and local or free LLM backends; a Jan 2026 fan tutorial lists Player2 and OpenRouter backends — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/97572-mount-blade-ii-mod-uses-chatgpt-procedurally-generate.html); [Exputer](https://exputer.com/news/games/mount-and-blade-2-bannerlord-mod/)
- Command by Voice: World Conquest (Side Meicai, $9.99, early access Jul 10, 2026): rule a nation by giving orders to an AI by voice or text instead of menus, using DeepSeek. MEASURED: Mixed, 52% positive of 23 reviews — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4314010)
- Warrior Quest (demo released Aug 29, 2026, solo developer): local offline LLM interprets free-text speech for diplomacy; dice rolls decide success; developer says the model is only a gameplay mechanic. MEASURED: 11 reviews, all positive (tiny sample) — [Steam demo page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5120060/)
- Search surfaced further unreleased or unchecked Steam entries (a 4X with an embedded offline LLM, app 5122860; a grand strategy game with typed natural-language commands, app 4418400, marked coming soon) but the pages could not be read, so they are not verified — [5122860](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5122860/)
- Research: LLM agents playing Diplomacy and Civilization V (Vox Deorum) exist as academic work, not shipped games — [arXiv 2411.09109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.09109v4)

### Inferences
- Both Warrior Quest and Command by Voice are close in spirit to the target design (model interprets, dice or rules decide), yet their evidence is 11 and 23 reviews. Not enough to call a result.
- The strongest mod audiences use typing/voice for talk, not for issuing orders; the evidence for order-giving by free text in strategy is thin.

### Gaps
- No Workshop subscriber count for VotC, no Nexus downloads for Mantella, no Civilization or Stellaris LLM-diplomacy mod found. No qualitative player-use breakdown (what players use mods for) beyond tutorials and press.

## 3. What players find fun and not fun about typing to a character

### Takeaway
The only qualitative sources I could read are curated store snippets, press, and one practitioner essay. Pattern: fun comes from a character reacting to what you said; unfun comes from slow replies, unclear limits, fragile rules and typing effort.

### Cited Findings
- Fun (opinion, store snippets and press): responses that feel "real", voice interaction, characters acting on conversation, novelty of steering an AI character — [Steam: Suck Up!](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2726370/Suck_Up/); [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/inzoi-is-what-happens-when-the-sims-meets-ai-and-the-results-are-gloriously-unstable)
- Not fun: slow loads, robotic replies (Bannerlord 2023 mod, Vaudeville); GPU cost and bugs (inZOI); provider errors and breaking updates (VotC); setup friction (Mantella, VotC need API keys, STT, TTS) — sources above
- Practitioner essay (Jul 2026, vendor author, anecdotal, no sample sizes): players stare at a blank box, retype, give up; one playtester spent about 45 minutes talking a shopkeeper out of gear then lost interest; three LLMs playing a guard each gave way within five turns; fix proposed is to separate deterministic logic from LLM phrasing and test restricted outcomes automatically — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/ashutosh-veriprajna/i-helped-build-ai-that-talks-back-in-video-games-infinite-freedom-nearly-killed-the-fun-5fa4)
- Guides recommend suggested conversation starters beside the box (hybrid input) to reduce blank-page paralysis (secondary summary, not data) — [Abratabia guide](https://www.abratabia.com/llm-npcs/dialogue-systems.php)
- Academic note: when an NPC can say anything, the designer must decide what it must never say — [arXiv 2510.25820](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25820)

### Inferences
- A model-ignores-rules failure (guard handed over a key) is the one that kills game meaning; your design (model parses only, authored replies) avoids it by construction.

### Gaps
- No systematic coding of Steam review text; no survey on typing fatigue. Review text was not accessible through the tools used.

## 4. Retention

### Takeaway
No published retention curve or developer median. The only hard evidence is Whispers' concurrency (peak 964 on Aug 15, 2025; about 5-7 average in mid-2026) and playtime averages of 2.5 h (Suck Up!) and 3.2 h (Whispers) from aggregators.

### Cited Findings
- Whispers peak and recent averages: [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/3730100/graphs); [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/3730100). Avg 3.2 h: [Whispers aggregator](https://datahumble.com/whispers-from-the-star-3730100)
- Suck Up! average 2.5 h: [Datahumble](https://datahumble.com/suck-up-2726370)
- Suck Up! recent reviews (64%, 14 in 30 days) show a thin trickle a year after launch — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2726370/Suck_Up/)

### Inferences
- Hours are comparable to a short novelty experience. Whispers' daily-time cap and review score suggest a "short, intense" model, not long retention.

### Gaps
- Aggregator averages are unaudited. No GDC or developer statement on novelty half-life found.

## 6. Attitudes to generative AI and labelling

### Takeaway
Disclosure is now common (about half of October 2026 releases) and a sizeable minority of players dislike it; the data say nothing specific about LLMs that only parse input.

### Cited Findings
- MEASURED (analysis of about 63,600 releases): 49.9% of October 2026 releases disclosed generative AI, 26.4% in October 2025; 13.2% for 2024 and 24% for 2025; 40.1% so far in 2026. A separate census of about 53,600 titles gives lower numbers (10.9%, 19.9%, 30.8%). Disclosure is far more common among tiny games (47% of 2026 titles with under 10 reviews vs 14% of those with 1,000+). Valve's Jan 2026 change narrowed disclosure to AI content players can see or hear, excluding behind-the-scenes tools — [Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/gaming/almost-half-of-all-new-steam-games-use-ai-now-3423933); [Games.gg](https://games.gg/news/steam-players-ai-games-survey/) (aggregators; datasets not verified)
- MEASURED survey (GameDiscoverCo, about 3,800 Steam players, Jun 25 to Jul 2, 2026, engaged-player skew): 43% no big issue with disclosure, 26% neutral, 31% negative (8% of those say they will not buy); 44% read disclosures in detail; only 17% think developers fully disclose AI audiovisual use. The survey did not ask about LLM NPC dialogue — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-do-steam-fans-really-think-about)
- An analyst finding (headline only; method not readable): AI stigma on Steam can cut a game's review count by about 53% and make its reviews more negative — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/data-analyst-finds-ai-stigma-on-steam-can-reduce-the-number-of-reviews-a-game-gets-by-around-53-percent-and-the-reviews-it-does-get-are-more-negative/)
- Backlash cases: Square Enix's AI tech preview became its worst-rated Steam game; Let It Die: Inferno peaked at 813 Steam players after a generative-AI controversy (headlines) — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/square-enix-releases-ai-tech-preview-that-instantly-becomes-its-worst-rated-game-on-steam); [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/24331/after-generative-ai-pr-disaster-let-it-die-inferno-peaks-at-just-813-players-on-steam)
- Whispers drew a "may divide gamers" framing yet scored Very Positive — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-launch-New-interactive-fiction-game-debuts-to-Very-Positive-reviews-may-divide-gamers-over-heavy-AI-integration.1087821.0.html)

### Inferences
- A label likely costs some reviews and goodwill, but the effect is largest for generated art and voice. A parser-only use is closer to the "coding/tools" category players tolerate (coding was the most-cited acceptable use in the survey) — an inference, not tested.

### Gaps
- No study separates LLM-as-parser from LLM-as-content-generator. Backlash headlines concern generated art or assets, not input parsing.

## 7. Design synthesis: what lasted and what players dropped

### Takeaway
Evidence for lasting praise is thin. Typed input earns praise when it opens a conversation with a reacting character, and fails when it is the only way to act. No source shows a free-text order interface beating buttons.

### Cited Findings
- Praised: talking to, convincing or deceiving a character (Suck Up!, Whispers, Warrior Quest); typed goals to steer characters (inZOI); NPCs acting on talk (Mantella) — sources above
- Weakly received: Command by Voice's order-by-text strategy (52% of 23); Vaudeville's investigation by chat (47% of 288) — sources above
- Dropped in favour of controls: no direct evidence found of players abandoning typing for buttons in these games.

### Inferences
- Plausible role for typing in a strategy game: questions and persuasion with characters (where an authored reaction is the reward), with buttons for routine orders and suggested lines beside the box. This is supported by pattern, not data.

### Gaps
- No evidence about compound instructions or question-asking specifically. No head-to-head data on typing versus click usage in any shipped game.
