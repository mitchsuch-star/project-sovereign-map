## 1. First ten minutes: what a brand-new player meets and must learn

### Takeaway
A new player is dropped from the main menu into a full 126-province campaign with a one-line greeting; the guided School of War lessons exist and are built, but are two optional menu buttons, not the default path. Orientation is spread over many surfaces (11 first-open lines, a Help/Keys/Terms/Orders book, a map key, hover tooltips); there is no forced or automatic first-turn guide in the main campaign that I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Main menu rows, in order: "The School of War — a guided campaign", "The School of War — The Table (a guided peace)", Continue, Load a Campaign, Settings, Quit; Begin is a separate row (a confirm-guarded "begin anew"). Source: `godot-client/project-sovereign/scripts/main_menu.gd` lines ~460-465 and 848-882 (checked in code). The School "REPLACES the backend's running world exactly like Begin" and choosing it is the consent (main_menu.gd ~901-911).
- The tutorial IS built, not only written. "The School of War" = Berthier's non-modal tutor card (`tutorial_overlay.gd`, CanvasLayer 90) on authored scenario `tutorial_1805.json`; the live script is the `STEPS` table in `tutorial_overlay.gd`; `docs/TUTORIAL_SCRIPT.md` section "The Danube Lesson (BUILT - POSITION 7, August 8, 2026)" says it mirrors it for design review. The doc's header says 20 cards after the Sept 23-27 refresh (I Situation ... XX Lesson Ends), "unbreakable" (per-step Skip chip, refused suggested order releases the step). A second lesson "The Table" (Peace of Pressburg, Dec 1805) was built in Pre-Deploy S14d part 2 (Oct 24, 2026): TUTORIAL_SCRIPT.md line ~431; `docs/TABLE_LESSON_SPEC.md`. The record says the Danube lesson's card I "now teaches Tab completion, province-click chips and the three first-contact doors". Whether the first player used either lesson before his 13-turn campaign: not stated in any record I read (gap).
- The Danube lesson's first beats are typed: turn 1 `economy`, then `Senarmont, move to Munich`, then `end turn`; turn 2 `Ney, defend` (to provoke a real objection). Source: TUTORIAL_SCRIPT.md table lines ~355-361. The record notes that the command line is disabled while the objection modal is up, so Trust/Insist/Compromise are buttons.
- Campaign boot text on the terminal: a banner "IMPERIAL HEADQUARTERS", "September 1805 - The War of the Third Coalition", "You are Napoleon Bonaparte." (`main.gd` `_show_welcome`, line ~1070, checked in code). Then the boot help (`_print_boot_help`): in the default folded state ONE line, "Your marshals await your orders, Sire." plus a link "▸ every command" (main.gd, docstring: Session UI-6, Nov 13). ### Inferences

### Takeaway

Recorded as landed; first-open/Score records show GE rows built (e.g. SCORE_FINAL: "The ending" pillar 7.50).

Output: Berthier unrolls the map with, when endings are armed, the designs clause + Congress clause + "Take provinces, keep the Emperor free and the marshals loyal, and make peace on your own terms. The Strategic Ledger (press T) keeps the score and the Cabinet (F1) holds every court", then "Today:" up to three counsel lines and the three doors. On an open-ended world it says "there is no laurel to be handed out; the war is the game." (checked in code.)

- Scores: GAME_REVIEW_2026_11_06 says first contact is 8.00-8.50 on the project's own instrument ("28 first questions, 0 shrugs") (`docs/audits/GAME_REVIEW_2026_11_06.md` table, line ~25).

- A player who never types "what's the goal" meets the win condition only via the Strategic Ledger/Cabinet/Dispatch news and the Congress summonable headline. This is a likely source of "disconnected" feeling, but no record states that the first player complained about goals specifically. ## 5. The typed line, precisely

### Takeaway
The typed line is a keyword/rule parser (the "fast parser", LLM off unless a key is supplied) that gives the game its identity per VISION.md; since the click interface exists, every ORDER also has a button and a click is faster, per the project's own 28-line measurement. Its worth, by the same record, is ASKING (free, state-reading questions), one-line compound or group orders, and forgiving typos; diplomacy is NOT typeable in the shipped client (redirected to the Cabinet).

### Cited Findings
**(i) Typed only / click only / both.**
- VISION.md: "Players don't click armies. They type commands" ("Marshal Ney, attack Wellington"), pitch "you talk to your generals - and they talk back"; disobedience-as-negotiation; design principle 4 "Every input gets a response. No silent failures." (`docs/VISION.md`.) CLAUDE.md line 3 repeats "Players type commands".
- Both: every executed order comes through the SAME `/command` executor. The click road writes typed command strings: region panel "Chips send the SAME typed commands a player would type - the executor owns..." (`scripts/region_panel.gd` header lines 10-17); PW-4 shows click orders were command strings (`march to` vs `move to`). The record says "Every ORDER has a button and a click is faster" (SYSTEMS_REFERENCE 228.6) and "Every act the player needs has a button; the line is the fast road, never the only one" (227.11).
- Click-only: all diplomacy. `main.gd` `_redirect_diplomatic_command` (line ~3953, checked in code): a typed sentence that is a diplomatic order is NOT sent; Berthier points at the Cabinet (F1) or the war table; the docstring says "since PC-X42 ... X45 every diplomatic verb has its Cabinet row, so none keeps a typed-only road" (ruling G1; user rule memory "diplomacy is never typed"). VALID_ACTIONS still holds ~15 diplomatic verbs, used by the backend/LLM/driver path. Not redirected: marshal reward verbs (rente/endow/commission), read-only advisories and questions, dialogue answers.
- Typed-only or typed-first (per the records; I did not exhaustively verify absence of a button for each): (1) QUESTIONS: no screen holds them in one place (228.6). ## 6. What VISION.md says the typed line was meant to be for the game's identity

### Takeaway
VISION.md makes the typed line the core innovation: "Players don't click armies. They type commands" and marshals answering by personality ("Disobedience as Negotiation"); the click interface and the plain-words effort came later and are not in VISION.md.

### Cited Findings
- `docs/VISION.md`: One-line pitch "A Napoleonic strategy game where you talk to your generals - and they talk back." Core Innovation: typed examples ("Marshal Ney, attack Wellington", "Davout, hold the line at Belgium", "Grouchy, march to the sound of the guns"); personalities Ney aggressive / Davout cautious / Grouchy literal; objections resolved by Trust / Insist / Compromise, which builds or destroys trust. "The Grouchy Moment" signature beat. Three layers of agency (advisors, marshals, nations). Target audience includes "Anyone who's ever yelled 'why won't my general just DO what I said'". Design philosophy: "LLMs explain, react, color - they never cause"; "Every input gets a response. No silent failures." Not: real-time, hex tactical, multiplayer.
- VISION.md's "Layer 1: ADVISORS (future - Phase 9)" is stale versus CLAUDE.md/records (Talleyrand and diplomacy are built); the doc header says "The north star".
- Tension recorded elsewhere: user ruling "diplomacy is never typed" (G1) moves the whole diplomacy layer to clicks; CLAUDE.md line 3 still describes "Players type commands ('Marshal Ney, attack Wellington')".

### Inferences
- The thing VISION.md calls unique (talking to generals) is today partly carried by popups/personality voice and Berthier's counsel, and the typed line is one of two input roads. A report can legitimately say the line's role has shifted from "the interface" to "the conversational/asking layer plus power-user shortcut".

### Gaps
- No record I read says what the developer wants the typed line's identity role to be after PW part 3; the parser-utility question he asked is answered in the project's 228.6/PW3-9, which is design-by-the-assistant under his "dont ask me questions use reason".
