# Row PW part 2 — the blind reading: the bar and the answer key (written BEFORE the readers read)

**The bar:** three fresh readers who have never seen the game are shown only the six frames below (the real
client at the first player's own setup, the final tree of part 2) and asked six questions. The bar is met
only if EVERY reader answers EVERY question correctly, by the key. A reader's "I cannot tell" on what the
key asks is a miss.

**Frames:** O a marshal's objection · L a letter that has arrived · R the map while the enemy's moves are
played on it · D the Cabinet's page for an enemy · F a battle with no winner yet · B the peace table after
one press of "Offer them more".

| # | Question | Correct means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | (O) A marshal objects to your order. In one line each: what happens if you press each of the five answers, and what does each cost or gain? | Trust Massena: he attacks Archduke John at Tyrol instead; +3 trust. Proceed as Ordered: he obeys your order; 2 actions, −10 trust. By My Word: he obeys; it costs 3 authority, not his trust. Compromise: a middle course (he takes an aggressive stance); +3 trust. Cancel the Order: nothing happens; nothing spent. |
| 2 | (L) What is Prussia asking for? What happens if you accept, and if you reject? | Open borders — each side's armies may cross the other's land. Accept: it is signed at once. Reject: nothing is signed; their court remembers the refusal. |
| 3 | (R) What is this frame showing, and what happened to Swabia? | The enemy's moves are being shown/played on the map ("THE ENEMY MOVES"; it can be skipped). Swabia: Bavaria (an ally) lost it to Austria. (Also on the frame: a battle at Milan with no winner yet.) |
| 4 | (D) What do the numbers under "What moves them" mean, and which one counts most against peace with Britain? What are "Diplomatic points (DP)" for? | Each number adds to, or takes from, their willingness to agree; the largest against is "Their relations with you" (−10). Diplomatic points pay for the Cabinet's acts (each act has a price in DP: e.g. 3 DP to offer peace to Britain alone, 1 DP to ask for a truce). |
| 5 | (F) What did the dice roll do in this battle, and how did luck affect each side? | Archduke Charles rolled 7 (+2 skill = 9): a strong roll — the attack hits 6% harder than an average one. Luck: France fights 4% weaker, Austria 2% stronger. |
| 6 | (B) What will "Ask for more" do if pressed now? What does "Review and sign" say will happen? What is the number on each court's row? | "Ask for more": you pay each court 100 less gold. "Review and sign": they will not sign it yet. The number is how willing the court is to sign; it signs at 50 (each stands at 0 of 50). |
