# Row PW part 2 — the blind reading, held again on the final tree: the bar and the answer key (written BEFORE the readers read)

**The bar (unchanged from the part's first reading):** three fresh readers who have never seen the game are
shown only the frames below (the real client at the first player's own setup, the final tree of part 2, after
the review's fixes) and asked six questions. The bar is met only if EVERY reader answers EVERY one of the six
correctly, by the key. A reader's "I cannot tell" on what the key asks is a miss.

**Frames (the bar's six):** O a marshal's objection · L a letter that has arrived · R the map while the enemy's
moves are played on it · D the Cabinet's page for an enemy · F a battle with no winner yet · B the peace table
after one press of "Offer them more".

| # | Question | Correct means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | (O) A marshal objects to your order. In one line each: what happens if you press each of the five answers, and what does each cost or gain? | Trust Massena: he attacks Archduke John at Tyrol instead; +3 trust. Proceed as Ordered: he obeys your order; 2 actions, −10 trust. By My Word: he obeys; it costs 3 authority and no trust. Compromise: a middle course (he takes an aggressive stance); +3 trust. Cancel the Order: nothing happens; nothing spent. |
| 2 | (L) What is Prussia asking for? What happens if you accept, and if you reject? | Open borders — each side's armies may cross the other's land. Accept: it is signed at once. Reject: nothing is signed; their court remembers the refusal. |
| 3 | (R) What is this frame showing, and what happened to Franconia? | The enemy's moves are being shown/played on the map ("THE ENEMY MOVES"; it can be skipped). Franconia: Bavaria (an ally) lost it to Austria; a battle there was lost. |
| 4 | (D) What do the numbers under "What moves them most" mean, and which one counts most against peace with Britain? What are "Diplomatic points (DP)" for? | Each number adds to, or takes from, their willingness to agree; the largest against is "Their relations with you" (−10). Diplomatic points pay for the Cabinet's acts (each act has a price in DP: 3 DP to offer peace to Britain alone, 1 DP to ask for a truce). |
| 5 | (F) What did the dice roll do in this battle, and how did luck affect each side? | Archduke Charles rolled 8 (+2 skill = 10): a strong roll — the attack hits 8% harder than an average one. Luck: France fights 2% stronger, Austria 5% weaker. |
| 6 | (B) What will "Ask for more" do if pressed now? What does "Review and sign" say will happen? What is the number on each court's row? | "Ask for more": you pay each court 100 less gold. "Review and sign": they will not sign it yet. The number is how willing the court is to sign; it signs at 50 (each stands at 0 of 50). |

## Two measurements beside the bar (declared before the reading; they do not count toward it)

The review's sibling sweep found prices on small buttons that are still not everyday words. Two frames are
shown with one question each, and **the rule is written first:** a measurement that two or more of the three
readers miss is FIXED in this session where the fix is local to that one surface (the general's card), and is
FILED as an owned row at the head of the next session where it is a vocabulary across the whole game (the
"AP" tags: 251 pins in 81 test files).

| # | Question | Correct means |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | (C — the general's card, beside the command desk) You clicked your general Ney and this card opened. What do the two numbers on the button "1 Hold · 2" mean? How many actions can you still spend this turn? | The 1 is the key that gives the order (its number); the 2 is its price in actions. 4 actions are left ("4 left" on the card; "4/4" on the desk). |
| 8 | (S — a province's panel) What does "(2 AP)" on the button "Fortify (2 AP)" mean? | It costs 2 actions (action points) of the turn's. |
