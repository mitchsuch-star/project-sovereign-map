## 0. In one paragraph

**The game is in a good state.** Sixty-three seeded driver campaigns (1,806 played turns), the benchmark's 36 arms (778) and the AI-France experiment's 30 runs (1,200) finished with no crash, no soft-lock and no traceback (this review's own grep of the 83 run consoles under `tools/playtest_runs`, which the archive does not keep); no run stopped on a blocker the game raised (the four Rhine-road arms record the driver's own answer-cycle stops), and the only gold the driver books as off the books is the announced treaty lumps it misclassifies (a harness row, §3.2); the ledger reconciles to the last coin for all eighteen courts over twenty-two turns; a 29-turn world round-trips through save and load with zero diverged keys; no float reaches the client in about 2,500 responses (roughly 700 in the turn reader's census, 1,790 in the scale reader's, 34 GET reads); the parser corpus passes 1,043 of 1,043 and 358 typed lines produced zero misreads. The benchmark reads **7.57 over 11 of 14 pillars** (October 5: 7.50), command up from a capped 6.00 to 8.00–8.50 and economy from 7.00 to 8.00. **The economy works at the scale anyone has played, and by its arithmetic beyond it:** the peaceful chest converges on its ceiling (about 32,000) instead of running away, courts spend their purses on armies, nobody goes bankrupt in sixty turns, and a turn costs 90–220 ms depending on the board, with no growth trend over 60 timed turns and bounded state over 120; what no script can reach is a France holding 45 provinces, where the code says conquest pays steeply and only the coalition brakes it (§3.2). **The enemy AI works** for war (it strikes after it declares — 78% of war turns show a visible attack — concentrates against a passive France and destroys it on four of six ambient seeds), for the table (a losing court sues, a winning one dictates, refusals cool down honestly, every diplomatic act has a UI control) and for laws (eleven effect types wired, no dead law, no lapse, courts enact 12–17 laws by turn 40). **The user's "fully autonomous France" question has a clear answer:** the courts' own AI, handed France, plays a brilliant opening (Austria's field army 121,000 → 0 by turn 6, Vienna stormed on turn 5 on nine seeds and turn 6 on the tenth) and then has no idea what to do — it marches to Estonia, never writes a letter, never enacts a law, and under the driver's default answers loses its satellites by refusing every petition (a steward's answers keep them) — and lands in **one outcome class on nine of ten seeds**. Nor does the passive world supply variety: ten ambient seeds tell one story with the clock moved. Both are the political layer working as designed (no dice in it, Tier-1 content fixed); "multiple unpredictable outcomes" is the one thing this review could not find, and §6 says what would make them. **The defects** are narrow: one Golden Rule 4 breach (a drilled corps keeps its +20% through every garrison assault), one soft-lock class (an exception inside `advance_turn` wedges the campaign forever), three unbraked siblings of a closed row (the same corps attacking the same target two or three times a phase), a case-mix bug in defiance, two silent drops on the command road, an objection that promises an illegal order, a coalition that brews every three turns and can never form, and a law rung that saves for a Staff two courts can never afford. Of the benchmark's five new red items, two are its own scripted arms drifting under the slices since October 5, one is a board change that is a design question, one is marginal, and one is the narration defect above.

## 3. The four questions

### 3.1 Does the game work — the played campaigns

**The commanded arm (France scripted at half strength, five seeds, `rev1106-cmd-*`).** Every seed finishes `completed`: 200 commands, 0 unknown blockers, 0 tracebacks, 0 OFF THE BOOKS rows on 240 turns (the five seeds and the insist arm). France ends turn 40 with 27 / 28 / 25 / 23 / 26 of its 28 provinces (historical / austerlitz / marengo / jena / ulm), spending 63–86 of 160 action points and having 58–80 of 200 orders refused honestly (stance locks, dead or captured targets, a court France has just signed with). ### 3.2 Does the economy work at scale

**Yes, within the campaign lengths anyone has played, and by its arithmetic beyond them — with two flat terms the user should look at.**

*Measured (play).* The commanded France run to sixty turns (`rev1106-econ-cmd60-historical`) converges: chest 24,363 at turn 40, 29,987 at turn 52 against a ceiling of 32,400, net decaying from +1,601 to +242 as the Charges climb from 1,415 to 2,771 a turn — the S14j rule holding a hoard at its ceiling, not a runaway. The Dispatch's forecast ("declare on turn 54") was out by one turn: four courts declared at 53 and the chest fell 1.2–1.8k a turn. `chest_window` reads 0 off the books on every turn of every arm but the Rhine-road ones, whose non-zero rows (−150, −124, −750) are announced treaty lumps the driver misclassifies (ECON-1 play, a harness row); those four arms also carry `unknown_blockers` entries that are the driver's own answer-cycle stops on the script's held clicks, not blockers the game raised. The courts: at-war Britain, Austria and Russia spend down to 4–6k by turn 60; the neutrals hoard to their own ceilings (the Ottoman at 26,335 against a ceiling of 27,020 — EAD-6's design); the courts' total 178k → 157k, under the 200k target; armies plateau at the recruit rung's 1.5× force-limit band (Sweden 78,140 against 78,750); the over-limit surcharge is graduated (5% at 1.12×, 16% at 1.5×, 25% at 1.7×); `bankruptcy_turns` is 0 for every court at turns 40 and 60. Manpower never binds — 14 of 20 courts sit at the 100,000 infantry cap at turn 60. ### 3.3 Would a fully autonomous France be cool, realistic, and varied — the AI-France experiment

**What was built.** No mode of the game lets the AI play France, so the fleet built one for the review: `tools/ai_france_sweep.py` (untracked; it imports `tools/ai_v_sweep.py`'s own RunCapture, derive_metrics and child_env, so the ambient schedule, the per-turn re-seed and `PYTHONHASHSEED=0` are the committed sweep's). Each turn, before the re-seed and `TurnManager.end_turn`, France's phase runs through the SAME objects the courts use: `EnemyAI.process_nation_turn('France', …)` (the military tree), `EnemyAI.execute_admin_phase('France', …)` (recruits, commissions, ships), and the incoming letters answered by the courts' own `diplomacy.calculate_acceptance` built from each dialogue's terms (69–85 letters a run scored that way; 4–12 with no scorable terms took a documented fallback). Because `_acting_nation` equals `world.player_nation`, the executor treats France's orders as the player's, and the player-facing surfaces — objections, capture choices, clarifications, the glorious-charge and last-stand questions, the hard-stop dialogues — are raised against the AI France and answered by a wrapper (trust / secure / the first option / restrain / fight to the last; a declaration confirm taken only when `war_council.can_declare_war`, the courts' own exposure calculus, allows it). Two arms: **main** (client petitions declined, rebellions accepted — the driver's defaults) and **steward** (petitions granted, a rebellion answered by investing). The control is the passive sweep on the same ten seeds; the determinism control (historical twice) is byte-identical; **0 phase errors and 0 harness errors across 20 runs, about 240 French commands a run through the real executor**. Two honest limits of the mode, both France-literal seams in the engine rather than in the harness: **an AI France never writes a letter of its own** (`ai_diplomacy.process_diplomatic_phase` returns None for the player, `ai_diplomacy.py:2518`), so it never sues for peace, never courts, never declares a war by design; and **it never enacts a law or decrees an edict** (`reforms.find_ai_enactment`, `reforms.py:1300`; `affairs.find_ai_edict`, `affairs.py:490`). A third seam the harness exposed: the vassal shore-up rung reads `nation_dp`, which France does not have (its points live in `diplomatic_points`), so an AI France can never invest in a satellite (AIF-2).

**The verdicts, in the user's three words.**
- **Cool: yes, for ten turns, and in flashes after.** The opening is the best Napoleonic campaign the engine has produced in this review — five corps, four battles in two turns, Vienna on turn 5, Bavaria protected on every seed where a passive France loses it on nine. The later drama is real and the records tell it: the march to Estonia, the satellites' revolt and their bribers named, Britain suing, the Emperor taken and ransomed by a peace. What never happens: a lost Paris, a Fall, a volte-face, a Confederation (one formation in twenty runs — Holland on ulm).
- **Realistic: no, after turn 8.** Austria is annihilated — not beaten, annihilated — by turn 6 and eliminated by turns 9–17 on eight of ten seeds, which no 1805 allows; the Russian deep raid has no object and only attrition to stop it; the sovereign marches as a line corps to the edge of the map; the AI never makes peace (the seam above), so its wars end only when the other side sues; it hoards 11–21k gold because the law rung is the player's; under the driver's default answers it loses its whole satellite web for want of a petition answered (the steward arm keeps it, so this is the answering policy plus the missing shore-up rung, not the tree); about 25 of its attack orders a run are refused by the executor for third-party soil it is at peace with (AIF-5 — the courts' target filter and the player's peace guard disagree; both refuters read it as a harness-side mismatch, PLAUSIBLE). **The absurdities are the rungs the courts use every day:** the undefended-capture rung with no notion of a front or of distance from home, and the admin chain with no steward's hand. They are the same rungs behind Archduke Charles's walk from Provence to Berry and Shrapnel's walk through Brittany on the commanded arms.
- **Multiple unpredictable outcomes: no.** Nine of ten seeds land in one class (Austria destroyed early, France at 41–50, satellites lost, the war with Russia never ended, Britain at war on five seeds and in truce on the others); the tenth differs by one garrison battle. The variance that exists is peripheral — when Austria falls, who bribes which satellite, whether the Emperor is caught once. The passive world (§2) is one story too, and the human script lands at 23–28 every time. Both are the political layer working as designed — deterministic, Tier-1 content fixed, the seed moving bars — and the military tree being a strong tactician with no strategist above it. The fleet's refuters were right to call this design rather than defect; it is also the plain answer to the question asked.

**What an AI France would need to be a mode** (recommend, not build — none of it is a defect of the human game): a court-side proposer for the player's nation (the one seam in `process_diplomatic_phase`, with a sue-for-peace rule), the player guards lifted in `find_ai_enactment` and `find_ai_edict`, the shore-up rung reading France's real points, a sovereign-risk rung that keeps the Emperor off the map's edge, the AI's attack filter reading the peace-soil rule the executor applies, answers for the moment letters and affairs (20–27 a run lapsed unanswered), and above all **a design for France** — the engine's own `imperial_designs` / `agendas` machinery driving the military tree's target choice, so that a march ends at Pressburg instead of Reval. That last is also the fix for the courts' own deep raids.

### 3.4 Does the enemy AI work — war, laws ("tech"), diplomacy

**War: yes, and better than the records say.** SFR-DR1 ("the league declares and rarely strikes") does not hold at HEAD: after every Fourth Coalition declaration the league attacks visibly on 6 to 8 of the next 8 turns (cmd-austerlitz 8 of 8, cmd-jena 8 of 9, cmd-ulm 6 of 8, econ-cmd60 6 of 7), and across the eight passive-France arms the player sees an enemy attack on 235 of 301 war turns (78%); where a court has not marched, the EAD-8 reading says why in public facts ("Britain has no road to us but the sea"). The benchmark's own AI-aliveness C6 reads the same seeds the other way — visible attacks on 10 of 33 and 15 of 37 war turns on the commanded austerlitz and marengo arms — because it counts every turn of a long war, including the stretches where the courts hold and the fog shows nothing, where the fleet counted the turns after a declaration; the weakest fleet arm, ulm, shows 21 of 40 with a seven-turn silence. The league strikes when it declares; it does not strike every turn. The military mind reads as sound in the code: cooldowns decay, the spoils-deferral cannot deadlock, retreats obey the movement law, no court reads the player's fog, every broad except reports, the admin whitelist covers every key all fourteen rungs return. What the review found are **three sibling rungs that escaped the repetition brake FA-N72 put on P0 and P4**: the jealous glory hunt (P3.9), the garrison assault (P4.25) and the stagnation breaker's "surrounded" strike let one corps hit the same target two or three times in a single phase — Cuesta twice on Wellesley on turns 10–11 of ulm and lodi, Mack three times on Paris on turn 25 of ulm, Moore three times on Paris on turn 16 of marengo, Deroy three times on Bennigsen — and the glory hunt drives the futility counter past its limit of 3 and keeps attacking (AIMIL-1, AIMIL-2, CMD-1; Rule 10's class). **Concentration of force** is single-marshal by construction: P4 prices one attacker and inflates his ratio by every co-located friend (+8% each, at full strength), but the resolver brings only adjacent, unfortified, non-literal, non-hostile corps by a roll — 26 of 68 candidates arrived in four seeds — so the AI's odds are more optimistic than what it gets (AIMIL-3, PLAUSIBLE). The enemy phase processes the courts in the fixed roster order every turn (AIMIL-5, a known deferred item) — one of the few things that never varies between seeds.

**Laws ("tech"): wired and stable, rote for the AI, and the one law that matters is out of two courts' reach.** Every effect type the 27 laws use has exactly one engine reader (manpower regen, recruit price and morale, drill morale, supply, satellite loyalty, blockade denial, the System's closure, force limit, staff actions, cures); no dead law, no lapse on any board, the quote equals the applied effect, the repeal text is honest, the validator refuses an unwired type. The player's side is legible and the Staff pays (France's Staff on ulm at turn 15; the chest crosses 9,000 at turn 14 there and turn 25 on historical). **The AI side:** Britain reaches its Staff on five of six ambient seeds (turns 27–33), Austria on four (27–40), **Prussia and Russia on none of the six** (on the commanded enacts-historical board Russia's Staff came at turn 39) — and the doctrine spec's T8 pass condition (two of four rivals cured by turn 30 on historical) is missed at HEAD: one of four (LAW-1 read). The arithmetic says why (READ-LAW): the purse bar for the Staff is price + 1,000 + 5 × (the whole slate's upkeep + 300), i.e. 12,500–13,000 once a court holds its cheap laws, against chest fixed points of 6,140 (Prussia) and 5,375 (Austria) at the boot economy; the "save for the Staff" rule has no reachability guard, so a court in the band buys nothing for the rest of the game (LAW-3 read); and the Edicts rung, armed November 2, drains 25–29k gold a board and ignores the saving rule (LAW-1 play). The spec recorded Prussia and Russia as "never, recorded not tuned"; the review's reading is that this is a bar set too high plus a leak, and that the courts' Decree in authority (AU-20's flip) leaves Prussia at authority 31 against a floor of 30 on every board. **As a tech layer it is a shopping list, not a tree:** five identical +25% regen laws, three identical +5 drill laws, three identical ×0.85 artillery laws, no mutual exclusion, no slot cap, no law that scales with authority or war; the real trade-offs are the Staff-plus-cure pair, Russia's Extraordinary Levy and the Porte's New Order. The courts buy drill laws they never use (Prussia issued 18 orders in 40 turns and 0 drills). §6 recommends the one addition.

**Diplomacy: it works and is mostly realistic.** The AI proposes sensible things at sensible moments — a losing court sends `armistice_losing`, a winning Austria that has taken Paris dictates 4,250–4,500 gold, Corsica and a Duchy of Normandy, neutrals ask small; it refuses France's peace while it holds French soil and signs only on its own offer (the Sept 1 observation "Austria signs while holding French provinces" is now the titled status quo by ruling, told on the confirm); Britain and Russia sign cheap stalemates because they hold nothing; refusal cooldowns are honest and escalate; a vassal refused twice rebels and its ally enters by the cascade. The AI-to-AI layer produces Congress peaces (Hanover pays Prussia 2,538 gold), sponsorship compacts that lapse and renew, a named BROKEN BARGAIN, allegiance auctions, revanche designs; the §4.2c budgets are right (intent asks and bandwagon letters never starve each other); every broad except in eight files reports; the descent exists (courts make peace without France). **Every diplomatic verb in `VALID_ACTIONS` has a UI control** — the user's rule holds on the ones checked. The characters: Austria the patient builder (it climbs the whole ladder to alliance on the courted arm and then threatens the Porte), Britain the paymaster who lands but never marches inland, Russia the arbiter in its ladder lines; **Prussia is the weak casting** — its design is Hanover in every arm, it is a member of none of seven leagues that formed, and it bandwagons on no one (DIP-1: the relation < −10 gate, with no Prussian statecraft hook, decides). The ladder announces "prepared to go as far as an ultimatum" about 25 times across eight seeds and issues none. Real defects: an eclipse coalition against a non-French hegemon brews on one qualifier that can never form and is re-announced every three turns, forever (AMB-1 / AIDIP-1 — a hegemon at alarm 97 never draws a league); the AI's settlement letter quotes an indemnity priced to the war that a bankrupt payer cannot pay, and the ratifier clamps it silently (AIDIP-2 — the ruling to price to the war is kept; the letter's honesty is the finding); `sell_neutrality` can offer seven gold for a binding compact (AIDIP-4); AI-to-AI refused asks recur on a fixed six-turn cadence with the ordered-pair cooldown written but never read (AIDIP-3).

## 6. Recommendations (recommend, not build)

**Before the build** — the defects of §4.1, in that order; by this review's estimate items 1–12 are each an afternoon or less, and three of them (3, 4, 5) are one sibling sweep. Then the two benchmark arms re-staged (§4.2) so naval and diplomacy read on the board that exists.

**Rulings the review asks of the user** — §4.3's first four rows: whether Europe's one story is the shipped contract or a Tier-2 band to widen; Prussia's casting; Britain's letter without contact; and the Staff two courts can never afford. Each has a default the review would take: widen one band (a second design for Austria and a deck-order band for Russia, the intent spec's own AI-0c/AI-0d mechanism) and measure the arm-C signature again; give Prussia a bandwagon hook that reads a collapsing France; let a closed-port Britain past a chosen exhaustion mark (the review's proposed default: 80) write without contact; and make the Staff's bar read the slate's upkeep once rather than five times for the Staff alone.

**Three expansions the evidence argues for** (ambitious, as the user prefers them; none is promised anywhere, so each needs an owner row and a landing slice under Rule 9 before it is mentioned to a player):
1. **A strategist above the tactician.** The military tree is a superb opening general and then a province-grabber. Give the courts' target choice a reading of their own `agendas` / `imperial_designs` — a front and a distance-from-home term — so that Archduke Charles stops at Lyon and an AI France stops at Pressburg. This is the single change that would make an AI-run France a mode worth shipping, and it would also end the deep raids on the commanded arms.
2. **Decks into trees.** Twenty-seven laws with no exclusions read as a checklist. A two-branch deck per court with one mutually exclusive choice and a prerequisite (a levée branch against a professional branch, the Staff as the second tier) would make the AI's deck order a character statement and give the lapse rule something to choose between; a standing-linked cap of three non-Staff laws in force does the same with less authoring.
3. **The middle layer of marshal drama.** The machinery (the glory clock, the UNMET line, the bond) exists; the marshal does not say it. Two objection lines per trigger per personality, an audience resolution that names what moved ("Murat's grievance is noted; his patience holds four turns"), and a commanded script that actually provokes defiance for the benchmark.

## 7. What this review did not do

The live-parser arm (`--llm anthropic`) by rule; the Godot client (UI/UX reads NOT EXERCISED; the user's S15 walk owns them); a human-driven campaign (every France here was scripted, passive or AI-driven — a human who spends the chest and garrisons the interior would end differently, and the commanded arm's 23–28 provinces are a floor, not a reading of play); the Humbled Peace (no arm); conquest at 45 provinces by play (no script survives the coalition that long; the arithmetic is in §3.2); A/B attribution of the commanded arm's move from 17 to 27 on historical; seeds lodi and rivoli on the ambient driver arms; the full test suite (green at this commit by the hook, not re-run); the full 1805 map (it does not exist yet — the scale findings are extrapolations from profiles at 126 provinces and 35 marshals).
