## 1. Verdict

This campaign shows a game whose **systems are markedly better than its reporting of them** — and, for the first time, a set of yesterday's fixes that made the reporting *worse in the direction that matters*. The underlying machinery earned its keep: an entire Britain–Spain war was fought, embittered (`REVANCHE`) and concluded without France; Bernadotte's trust ran 40 → 0 across fifteen turns of unrewarded victories and ended in a refusal that named its own cause; the muster preview taught me the exact five words that would fetch Soult and then honoured them. But the CA9 through-line — *every system computes the right answer and then tells the player a different one, and the divergence always points the way that makes them commit* — is not closed; it has migrated. Three of the five P1s in this report are **regressions introduced by fixes landed on Aug 9**: the honest-availability build for the grievance channel is overwritten at the delivery seam by the July-25 AP refresher; the cautious-attacker confirm gate the user commissioned **never armed once in fifteen turns** because it reads an odds band computed from an optimistic figure; and CA9-N5's own rewrite of the dialogue failure string silently killed the fall-through branch that let a typed order escape a hard stop. Two more P1s are older and were simply never played long enough to hit: the war HUD kept naming Hesse as an enemy for twelve responses after France signed peace with it, and a redemption event — an entire gameplay branch — is destroyed outright by any higher-precedence popup on the same response, with no stash and no recovery. Directional pillar movement is **≈6.4, down from ≈6.9**, and every point of that drop is trust in what the game says, not in what it does.

### The five P1s, mechanism by mechanism

**PT-66 — the war HUD outlives the peace.** When rows are collapsed into one multi-participant war, `opponent_display` is built from every nation on the enemy side of `side_by_nation`, with no check that the nation still has a live WAR pair with France. `side_by_nation.pop` (`settlement_helpers.py:1104`) only fires when a nation has *no* remaining active pair anywhere — Hesse kept `Hesse|Holland`, so it stayed. Measured: peace ratified at rec 95 (`previous_state: WAR → new_state: PEACE`), and rec 95 through rec 107 — twelve consecutive responses — still render `Britain + Austria + Hesse + Russia`. Two lines below, the **score** is aggregated over `row_opponents` only (the pairs that actually produced a WAR row), so the name list and the number on the same card are computed over different belligerent sets. The `[A-F5]` comment shows the author solved exactly this for the score and not for the name. Simultaneously, recs 80–96 carry a `status: war` row naming Austria *and* a `status: armistice` row for Austria; `war_status_panel.gd:268` renders the first and `:350` the second.

**PT-67 — the redemption branch is deleted at the moment it is offered.** `_route_response_ui` walks `_post_hud_response_routes` and returns on the **first** match. `incoming_proposal` sits at index 4; `redemption_event` sits **last**. At rec 89 the response carried both an `awaiting_redemption_choice` state for Bernadotte (trust 2) and Denmark's standing non-aggression pact — so the client raised the pact and dropped the redemption. It never appears again in any of the following 19 responses. There is no recovery: `check_redemption_threshold` returns `None` forever once `redemption_pending = True` unless trust climbs back above 20 (from 2); `pending_redemption` is not a PopupQueue slot; and although a `GET /pending_redemption` endpoint exists at `main.py:3019`, **`api_client.gd` never calls it** — its only redemption verb is `send_redemption_response` POSTing to `/respond_to_redemption`. The client already carries three purpose-built stashes for this exact failure class (`_stash_proclamation`, `_stash_envoy_digest`, `_stash_diorama`), whose own comment reads *"anything that drops the response here loses the moment permanently"*. Redemption has none. The collision is not rare — an unanswered envoy rides the passthrough on every response until it lapses.

**PT-68 — the price on the button is 20% wrong, in the tier where it matters most.** `objection_dialog.gd:141` renders `insist_button.text = "Proceed as Ordered (%+d trust)" % insist_penalty`. The engine applies that `insist_penalty` (−15) at `disobedience.py:1345`, then — for STRONG/EXTREME concerns, i.e. exactly where the defiance roll runs — `meta_executor.py:1738-1740` calls `apply_defiance_outcome(marshal, "failed_roll", world)`, which applies a further `marshal.modify_trust(-3)`, and sums both into `trust_change`. The failed-roll branch never writes `"defiance": True` (that key is only set in the roll-*succeeds* dict at `:1714`), and `main.gd:3198` gates the "DISCIPLINE HELD" / "Trust -18" disclosure on `response.get("defiance", false)` — so the branch that would show the extra −3 is unreachable on this path. At rec 87→89 Bernadotte was at trust 17: at the quoted −15 he lands at 2; at the real −18 he lands at 0 and fires the redemption event that PT-67 then destroys.

**PT-65 — the gate the user commissioned reads a number that defeats it.** `_build_muster_preview` builds `will_join_marshals` from `_muster_reason`, a deterministic eligibility ladder (broken / fortified / literal / hostile / grievance) that never consults arrival. It feeds that list straight into `_committed_reinforcement_strength` — `α · strength · effectiveness · attack_mod · rel_scale`, a pure strength haircut with **no arrival term** — and passes the result to `inferred_attack_odds_band`. Arrival is a *separate* roll in the resolver: `score = _calculate_arrival_score(...)`, `threshold = 60 if has_explicit_order else 65`, `arrived = score > threshold`. `muster_gate_arms` then keys on the band. Rec 98 verbatim: `MUSTER — Davout (18,874; 39,240 if all march) vs ArchdukeCharles (31,241 men) at Munich — the balance of force looks favorable.` … `WILL JOIN — Murat` — then `[Combat] Davout attacks cautiously at unfavorable odds alone.` and, in the same payload, `Murat could not reach the battlefield in time.` / `Massed effective strength: 18,874 (lead) + 12,806 committed (Lannes) = 31,680.` A 24% over-promise, in the direction `_defender_muster`'s own docstring warns about — *"the resulting error always pointed at 'favorable', which is the direction that makes the player commit."* CA9-F1 fixed that for the defender's term; the arrival term reintroduces it on the attacker's.

**PT-17 — the honest-availability build is undone at the delivery seam.** `_command_option` is explicit about its purpose: *"Every gate below MIRRORS a refusal `_execute_strategic_command` will itself raise, so the card can never offer something the executor is about to decline (and, worse, charge for)."* It returns `enabled: False` + `unavailable_reason`, but also carries `ap_cost: 2`. `refresh_petition_affordability`, run unconditionally on the petition at `main.py:1297-1304`, branches only on `ap_cost is None` — every priced option gets `option["enabled"] = ap >= cost` (`:1627`) and, when affordable, `option.pop("unavailable_reason", None)` (`:1635`). It never ANDs with the authored value. `_command_option` is the **only** `ap_cost`-bearing option anywhere in the backend that can be disabled for a non-affordability reason; the July-25 refresher's contract was safe until commit `a475fde` (Aug 9) introduced the first such option. Result on screen: a live gold *"Give him a command [2 AP]"* button contradicted by its own grey sub-label (`marshal_petition_dialog.gd:126-136` renders `detail`). Pressing it costs 0 AP but **destroys the petition** — `handle_petition_response` nulls `world.pending_marshal_petition` and clears the queue slot at `:1954-1957` *before* dispatching, and `_apply_command_choice` re-derives at `:2003-2006` and returns `success: False` with no restore. The `(pair, level)` latch is written at build time (`:806-815`), so that audience never re-fires. The slice's own 8 `TestHonestAvailability` assertions read `world.pending_marshal_petition` directly and never pass through the refresher — **the guarantee is pinned only at a layer the player never receives.**

### The three highest-leverage changes

1. **Make the delivery seam subtractive, and stop popping state before the arm succeeds.** In `refresh_petition_affordability`, only ever downgrade: `option["enabled"] = bool(option.get("enabled", True)) and ap >= cost`, and pop `unavailable_reason` only when affordability was the sole gate. In `handle_petition_response`, don't null `world.pending_marshal_petition` until the chosen arm returns `success: True`. Then re-site the 8 `TestHonestAvailability` assertions through the refresher (or the `/command` payload) so they bind where the player reads. *This restores the flagship arm of the row that landed yesterday and closes the pattern that produced it.*
2. **Weight each `will_join` contribution by its arrival probability before the band is computed.** One number fixes three things: the preview stops over-promising by 24%, "favorable" and "unfavorable" stop meaning opposite things on one screen, and the cautious-marshal gate starts arming in the fights it was commissioned for. Fix `combat.py:424`'s inverted `" alone"` in the same commit.
3. **Give the dispatch and the enemy phase a line budget, and render `diplomatic_events` in `main.gd`.** Collapse `turn_events` by family at the view seam exactly as IGR-B already does for the campaign log ("supply cost you 2,027 men at Tyrol and Milan"), collapse the per-court fog lines into one sentence naming the courts, and paste `dispatch_view.gd`'s ~14-line diplomatic block into `_display_morning_dispatch`. That last one moves every agenda shift, revanche, subsidy switch and third-party peace from a screen nobody opens to the one the player reads every turn — it is the cheapest aliveness in the report.
