# Row IC, Session 2b — the task to prove himself: the blind reading (November 20, 2026)

**This page was written BEFORE any frame was shot.** The questions, the answer key and the bar stand as they
are below; the result is filled in under "The result" after the readers have answered, and nothing above that
heading is changed once a frame exists.

The frames are the real client at the first player's own setup (Interface Scale 1.8 on a 5120 x 1377 window:
a logical view of 2844 x 765), shot by the instrument (`tools/dd_b_play.py`,
`DD_B_UI_SCALE=1.8 DD_B_WINDOW=5120x1377`, staged with `DD_B_STAGE=1`) on the 1805 campaign.

**The staging** (decided before any frame, so that the two men are asked on the same ground): Ney and Davout
both stand in Franconia, beside Austrian Bohemia, and each is brought to his audience in turn (the staging
command `debug audience <marshal>`: his trust set to 15 and the game's own question raised). Nothing else on
the board is arranged.

## The bar

Three fresh readers (Sonnet) who have never seen the game are shown only the frames named for a question and
asked the question. The bar is met only if EVERY reader answers EVERY asked question correctly by the key.
"I cannot tell" on what the key asks is a miss. A miss is reported as a miss.

## The questions

Question 1 is the one the plan names (`docs/INDEPENDENT_COMMAND_SPEC.md` §13, R-READ, "Version 6", in Session
2's form). Questions 2 to 4 read the two rulings the user gave in this session's Plan Mode ("No call-back",
"He presses on") off the same screens.

## The answer key (written before the frames)

1. **Because they are different kinds of men, and each proposes what suits his nature.** The windows say so in
   their first words: Ney "is a bold man, so he asks to" go for the enemy (take the nearest place the enemy
   holds — or, where the enemy in reach is too strong for him, hold and strike any enemy next to him that he
   outnumbers); Davout "is a careful man, so he asks to hold" where he stands "and to dig in". Right if the
   reader says the difference comes from the two men's natures as the windows state them (bold against
   careful). Wrong: "they stand in different places", "it is random", "you chose it", or no reason.
2. **No — neither.** The window says: "Until it ends he takes no orders, and you cannot call him back." Right
   if the reader says he cannot be called back AND takes no other order. "You can cancel it" is a miss.
3. **After three turns — later if he keeps winning.** The window says he carries it out "for 3 turns", and "If
   he ends his last turn on a victory he presses on, one turn at a time — 6 turns at most." Right if the reader
   gives the three turns AND says it runs longer while he wins (up to six). "After three turns" alone is a
   miss: the roll is half of the answer.
4. **Nothing.** His card says he is on a task to prove himself (with the turns left) and takes no orders until
   it ends; every order button on it is greyed. Right if the reader says no order can be given him now (and
   that he is on his task). Naming any order as available — "Cancel order" included — is a miss.

## The result

**The bar is met — in each of four rounds: twelve fresh readers in all, forty-eight answers, forty-eight right
by the key.** Each round is three fresh readers who had seen neither the game nor an earlier round, shown only
the frames named for a question (the whole screen and a crop, read from a folder outside the repository so that
this page could not be opened). The frames of each round are beside this page: `round1/` … `round4/`, and
`final/` for the screens as they ship.

Four rounds were read because the first, though it met the bar, was not good enough: **every reader of round one
answered question 4 right and every one of them then said they could not tell whether the greyed order buttons
on Ney's card still worked** — "7 Cancel order" among them, under a window that had just said "you cannot call
him back". A screen that is read right with that doubt is rebuilt, and a rebuilt screen is read again by a panel
that has not seen the old one.
