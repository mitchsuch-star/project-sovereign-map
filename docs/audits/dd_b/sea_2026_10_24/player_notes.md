# Player notes - the sea road (Ink & Iron, 1805 campaign)

**What I tried.** I played France and took the sea road: break the blockade, raise Ireland, carry the war across the Channel. The Admiralty book (button "46 sail - BLOCKADED") taught me the rules: marching 40,000 men into the four coast provinces for two turns "stages the camp", Britain recalls her squadrons and the blockade lifts. That worked: the blockade broke on turn 5 and again on turn 8, trade recovered, and the Mediterranean crossings opened. I then used the camp as a base. Dumonceau's small Dutch corps slipped past the Royal Navy (73 in 100) and took Munster in Ireland on turn 6; I secured it. I later landed him in Wales (69 in 100), where Moore (40,000 from London) beat him within a turn and he fell back to East Anglia. I never found a way to hold a British province. Meanwhile I beat Austria on land (Mack destroyed at Munich, Archduke Charles and John beaten), took an armistice, and then a peace by improving relations. Spent the gold surplus (it reached 27,000) on keels, the Temple of Glory, Road III (the Continental System) and client petitions.

**How far I got.** Turn 21 reached (20 turns ended). My navy was shattered on turn 12 by my own Grand Diversion (49 -> 25 sail, Britain lost 4), so the descent across the Channel was never possible; London held 100,000 British men in any case. On turn 14 Britain asked for an armistice and on turn 15 offered a white peace that "titles" Munster. I ratified it, so France ends the run holding Ireland's Munster by treaty, at peace with everyone, the coalition dissolved, Road III proclaimed but dead (0 of 26 ports shut, no clause in the peace). I only learned afterwards (Cabinet -> Formable Nations) that a free Ireland needs Ulster as well as Munster held at a settlement table while still at war with Britain; the peace I ratified closed that road.

**The three things that confused me most.**
1. **Orders that do things I did not order, or undo earlier work, without warning.** "Massena, march to Milan" ran through enemy Tyrol and became a full battle (corps system pulled in six marshals, alarm +8, pending armistice). Marching Soult and Napoleon from Flanders toward Normandy took them out of the camp via Picardy and threw away the staging. Neither the order's reply nor the card said so. The muster's "expect about 118,448" also overstated what arrived by more than half.
2. **The Grand Diversion's real price.** The card says 50 in 100 and "she is caught coming home and fights at readiness 65". It does not say that failing destroys about half the fleet and half my allies' fleets. I took the coin flip and lost the navy the whole road depends on.
3. **The Free-Ireland road is hidden.** Munster is the only Irish landing the Admiralty offers. Ulster, the second province, and the "carve it at the table while at war" rule appear only on the Formable Nations page. The peace review, the war panel and the region panel never mentioned it, so I traded the road away for a plain white peace. Related: the Active Wars panel renamed the whole war "War for Munster", and a Talleyrand mission to Austria kept running for four turns after the peace until I found its cancel row.

Smaller friction: header gold/income figures lag one command and swing hugely (+828 -> +13 after one battle, explained only in the Economy tab); the Morning Dispatch is printed before and shown after interrupts, so it contradicts itself; "Murat, cancel" is not understood ("halt" works); the "quiet turns" count in the dispatch jumped from 6 to 16 in a turn.
