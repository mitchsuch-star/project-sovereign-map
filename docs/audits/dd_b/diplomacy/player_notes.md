# Player notes: the diplomacy-first road (1805 campaign)

**What I tried.** I meant to end the Third Coalition's war at the table. On turn 1 the table was shut. A white peace with Austria turned into a whole-war settlement that every court refused (-8/50). A separate peace was blocked because my ally Bavaria was still at war with Austria. The armistice was rated "Hopeless". Talleyrand's advice was the honest one: win a battle first. So I fought only to get a seat. Mack was captured at Swabia on turn 2, and Archduke Charles was broken at Franconia on turn 4. Meanwhile I courted Prussia with Talleyrand, accepted every open-borders and non-aggression envoy, and bought off Sweden's design. On turn 4 I worked the settlement screen. I dropped Britain and Russia and eased Austria with 400 gold, which took her from 37/50 to 53/50, and ratified a separate peace. After that the road was all diplomacy:
- vassalized Bavaria;
- turned Prussia from neutral into a full ally;
- sponsored Prussia's claim on Hanover;
- accepted a British armistice on turn 9.

On turn 10 Britain offered a white peace that also covered Russia, and I ratified it. The whole coalition war ended at the table on turn 10. Threat fell from 100 to 48.

**How far I got.** I reached Turn 21 (Late July 1806) at peace with every great power. My allies were Prussia (relations 100) and Spain, with Bavaria, Holland, Italy and Switzerland as clients. I had bought off the designs of Britain, Austria (twice) and Russia. Treasury was 31,889 gold. The Congress of Paris, the diplomatic win, stayed out of reach. I had 38 of 45 titled provinces. Its table says Britain, Russia and Austria each need roughly 15 turns of courting plus an alliance. Talleyrand also stayed locked on the Prussia mission I could not end. The second half of the campaign was ending turns, answering petitions and buying off designs, with gold piling up and nothing on the peace road to spend it on.

**The three things that confused me most.**
1. **Diplomacy controls that do something other than what they say.**
   - On the Spain vassal proposal, "Use Talleyrand's suggestion" applied the harsh terms he then objected to. "Modify Proposal" still sent them (the P1) and spent 3 DP.
   - "Not Now" opened the objection instead of closing.
   - "Propose peace" from the assessment did nothing at all.
   - The Recall button writes a typed order the game refuses ("matters of state are conducted at the table").
   - The help and the Congress table tell you to type "buy off ..." orders that Berthier then refuses.
2. **The alliance pulled me into a war I never declared.** The full alliance with Prussia listed only open borders and a payment. One turn after I made peace with everyone, France "joined Prussia's war against Hanover, honouring their alliance". Hanover's page then said only the war leader can settle it, so I could not leave.
3. **I learned what I needed only after acting.**
   - The muster (who will march, who resents whom, "order Soult, support Ney") came with the battle result, not before. The one time I followed it ("Soult, support Ney"), Soult still sat out.
   - Supply limits were never warned at the order.
   - "Ease Russia" (France pays gold) lowered Russia's acceptance, with the reason "the terms claim a victory".
   - Bavaria's claim to Tyrol arrived as a petition only after I had ratified.
   - Buying off Austria's design was followed the next turn by a new design that cost another 1,128 gold.
