# Player notes — the sea road (DD-B, dd_b_sea)

**Where I got to:** Turn 21 (Late July 1806), 407 actions, 28 rows filed (1 P1, 9 P2, 18 P3).

## What I tried

I started the 1805 campaign from the title screen and went to the Admiralty, through the "45 sail · BLOCKADED" button. It is a clear page. It names the three sea orders (the blockade, the Grand Diversion, the expedition), what each needs, and the odds. My plan was the Descent. I marched Soult and the Dutch contingent to the Channel camp and laid down a keel every turn. Meanwhile the land army beat Austria: Mack and both Archdukes were captured, and Vienna was stormed. The blockade cost the fleet 5 readiness a turn (69 → 50), and nothing I tried stopped it. So the Grand Diversion fell to 25 in 100, and a failed one meant a fleet action at readiness 40. I never risked it.

I took the Irish road instead. I commissioned Oudinot (a 5,000-man corps) and landed him in Munster at 76 in 100, then landed Dumonceau in Ulster at 74 in 100. Both landings succeeded, which was the high point of the campaign. I spent a long time trying to get "Erect Ireland from Britain's lands" accepted at the settlement table, but it never reached Britain's 50. Then Britain asked for a truce, which lifted the blockade, and the next turn offered a status-quo peace. That peace gave France Munster and Ulster "titled by treaty". After it, readiness climbed every turn, from 50 to about 92, and with 2 keels a turn the fleet grew from 46 to 71 sail. Austria made peace and joined the Continental System. Russia took Vienna back during a truce and then made peace. The Congress of Paris stalled at 39–41 of the 45 titled provinces it needs, and I could not find a road to the last few.

## The three things that confused me most

1. **Separate peace was a dead end.** The settlement table said "Britain — Will sign (55/50)" and gave me "Separate peace with Britain". The next screen scored the same terms "0 — REJECT" and refused to send them, because my ally Spain was still at war with Britain. The same thing happened with Austria, because of Bavaria. The Austrian peace I did ratify then handed French Franche-Comté to Bavaria without warning. Spain's vassal offer was similar. It is labelled "Favorable", but its only drafts demand 700–800 gold a turn at ~0% acceptance, and "Modify Proposal" sent it anyway.
2. **Orders I never gave, and losses I only learned about afterwards.** Every muster preview appeared only after the battle had been fought: Massena at Tyrol, Lannes at Moravia, and whole corps marching to the guns. After the British truce, Dumonceau left my Irish garrison on his own and walked home through Scotland. After the peace, Oudinot faced internment in Munster, a province the treaty had just made French. He could not embark there, and his road home ran 9 marches through Britain.
3. **The sea rules shifted, and questions got no answers.** "No army is staged" stayed on the page even with a 61,000-man camp, and nothing explains what "staging" means. What a successful diversion opens changed from turn to turn: "both turns", then "first turn only", then "shut", then "second turn only". Each time I asked a sea question ("how do I break the blockade", "how can I raise readiness"), the game dumped the whole help into a command window that shows about four lines. "help navy" was not understood, and neither was pooling a fleet with Spain.
