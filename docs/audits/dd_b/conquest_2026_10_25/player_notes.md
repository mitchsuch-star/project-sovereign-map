# Player notes: conquest road (Ink & Iron, 1805)

## What I tried

I took the road "win the war in the field, take Vienna" and played it as a new player would: ask Berthier first ("who am I fighting", "what can I do", "what if Ney attacks Mack"), then order. Turn 1 I smashed Mack at Swabia with two attacks by Ney (the corps system pulled the whole army in), then scouted Tyrol and Vienna. The Archduke Charles (unscouted, 54k) took Milan's side and then Munich, which I retook on turn 3; I broke Charles and John at Tyrol on turn 4, marched on Vienna through Bohemia, beat the field army at its gates on turn 6 and took the capital by three assaults on turn 7 (Davout, Ney, then Murat to give him the glory). Massena took Hungary and captured Archduke Charles the same turn. I refused Austria's armistice on turn 5 and accepted its peace on turn 8 (Vienna, Hungary, Bohemia, Tyrol kept by treaty), then Russia's white peace on turn 9 and Britain's on turn 19. Between times I answered envoys (open borders, petitions from Italy, Switzerland, Holland), granted Prussia a licence against Hanover, enacted the Grand Quartier General and the Train des Equipages, rewarded marshals, tried to stage the Channel camp for a Descent and sent one expedition (it was intercepted: 4,244 men and 26 sail lost). I reached Turn 21 at peace with every court.

## How far I got

Turn 21. Vienna taken on turn 7, every war ended by treaty, treasury 15k+, the army worn from about 150k to about 120k by supply and battle. The Descent never came off: the camp staged on turn 18 but the Admiralty said no window could open (Spain's sail left when she made peace with Britain).

## The three things that confused me most

1. **Orders that quietly keep acting.** "Support Napoleon" is a standing follow-the-leader order, but its text only says the marshal will answer the guns if Napoleon leads a battle. Ney, Lannes and Davout followed Napoleon to Dresden when I sent him home alone (row 8). Massena abandoned his support order and "rushed to Hungary" at 36% morale without my word (row 7). A march to Flanders took Soult through Berlin and cost me 25 points with Prussia, with no warning at the time of the order (row 10).
2. **Supply is the real enemy and the warning comes late.** The muster does print "Swabia feeds 60,000 ... would lose ~4,589 men a turn", but the corps system then drags everyone into the battle province and nobody can move until next turn, so I paid for it anyway (4,447 men at Swabia, 6,541 at Munich, 4,566 at Hungary). I only learned that I could spread the army, and that a law cures the flaw, after several turns of losses.
3. **Messages that point at each other or at the wrong place.** The war banner is "on the left" but sits bottom right (row 1); "Not Now" on an envoy locks the Cabinet (row 6); Dumonceau recruiting sends me in a circle (row 11); the Admiralty book disagreed with itself about where a corps embarks (row 9); retaking Munich counted as "Captured an enemy capital +15" and put the alarm at 100/100 (row 5).

## Smaller things

The playable map is a strip in the middle of a very wide screen and the region panel opens in a corner box with its useful part below the fold (row 3). A letter typed into the command line when I pressed the G key to close Generals (row 4). The marshal rivalry system (Murat, Ney, Bernadotte, Soult) is rich and clear in its dialogs, and the peace preview, the "what if" muster, the licence confirmation and the Moments of History letters were the best-explained parts of the game.

## Tools

No tool failures. Two small notes: `act ... "type ..."` and `look` replies are cut at about 1,000 characters in the console summary (I read the state files for the rest), and the "map pieces" centres printed for stack labels are not the clickable icon (row 12).
