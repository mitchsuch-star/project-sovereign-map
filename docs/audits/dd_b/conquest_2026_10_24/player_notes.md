# Player notes - the conquest road (20 turns, turn 21 reached)

**What I tried.** I began the 1805 campaign, asked "what can I do", and attacked Mack with Ney on turn 1; the corps system pulled the whole army in and I won two battles in one turn. By turn 2 I had destroyed the Austrian main army (Archduke Charles captured), by turn 3 I had assaulted Vienna three times (Massena, Murat, Massena) and taken it, and by turn 4 I held Bohemia, Hungary and Carniola as well and had captured both Archdukes. On turn 5 I signed the Treaty of Vienna (kept four provinces), on turn 8 the Russians offered a white peace and I took it, and on turn 14 Britain offered peace and I took that too. I then went looking for more conquest: I enacted the Staff law and the supply train (the one-order "army" march was a great tool), declared war on Hanover (Talleyrand warned the alarm would jump from 45 to 73; I went ahead), took its last province, and watched a nine-court league form exactly as the Balance of Europe page forecast. On turns 16-20 the Fourth Coalition came: I beat Kutuzov and Charles at Moravia, then lost the war on the ground to Bagration, whose 46,000 men beat my morale-0 corps one after another (Lannes, Bernadotte and Massena captured, Murat destroyed, Napoleon cut out with 438 men).

**How far I got.** Turn 21 (the counter reads "Turn 21 - Late July 1806"). I held Vienna's title for most of the game, finished with an army in ruins, Moravia lost, an armistice with Austria, a defensive alliance with Prussia, and no end to the war with Russia and the league. Thirteen rows filed (0 P1, 6 P2, 7 P3). Row 5 was filed against action 97 and row 8's frame is action 156; row 5's frame is a turn-4 frame from the same turn as the real event (I mis-numbered it).

**The three things that confused me most.**
1. *Money and supply were invisible until they hurt.* The first muster preview warned about materiel but not that corps standing on allied Bavarian soil are paid by contract (-1,776g a turn). My treasury went from +828 to -480 in one turn and only the Ledger's Economy book explained why. Later the same shape repeated: crowded provinces, supply shortage and morale were reported in the turn log after the damage, never before the order.
2. *Diplomacy screens that trap or mislead.* "Not Now" on an envoy blocks the whole Cabinet until I answer it; "Let Talleyrand haggle" never produced a counter in four tries; "Adjust terms" on a peace preview opened a "discuss concessions" wizard that looked like I was giving territory away, with no Back button and Escape dead; and the whole-table peace with the coalition offered 100 gold to every court while Austria and Russia stayed on "settlement legitimacy" with no stated way to fix it. Most of the rest of the diplomacy (the Balance of Europe forecast, Talleyrand's warning before the Hanover war, the Congress terms) was excellent and honest.
3. *Peace and war have consequences nobody mentions at the signing.* Peace with Austria left the Russian armies sitting safely on Austrian soil beside Vienna ("cannot march - the field is on soil we are not at war with"); the Hanover war ended on the first capture because Prussia had already taken the rest; and the corps system, wonderful on the offensive, dragged my exhausted corps into every one of Bagration's attacks in turn. A "your corps are at 9% morale and an unseen army is adjacent" warning before End Turn would have saved the campaign.

**Smaller friction.** Escape dismisses a battle replay, then (pressed again) opens the pause menu; the command line goes dead the moment a petition pops up mid-sequence; the "Murat/Lannes seek an audience" petitions drain action points; routes are shown only after a long march order commits; the "unfavourable" verdict for 2.5:1 odds in the muster.

**Tools.** Everything worked except one transient crash of `act` (a PermissionError reading a state JSON while it was being written, turn 5).
