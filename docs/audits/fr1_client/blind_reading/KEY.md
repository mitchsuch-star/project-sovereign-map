# Row FR, Session FR-1 — IC3-N1's blind reading: the key and the bar (written first)

**Written November 23, 2026, before any frame was captured and before any reader saw anything.**

**The row:** IC3-N1 (`docs/BUG_FIXES.md`). Row IC Session 3's second blind reading left three words unread on
screens older than that session:
- "actions" was never defined where it is counted;
- the dashed buttons were not known to be buttons;
- "titled provinces" was unexplained.

The row is done when a fresh blind reader at the first player's setup reads all three right.

**The readers:** three fresh Explore agents (Sonnet), each reading the same crops of frames taken in the real
client at the first player's setup (Interface Scale 1.8 on 5120 × 1377). Hovers are shown only where a frame was
taken with the cursor resting on the control. No reader sees this key, the code or the other readers' answers.

**The questions:**
1. What is an "action" in this game, and what spends one?
2. The order typed under the line: how many actions does it cost, and how many do you have?
3. What happens if you press one of the gold "[+ …]" chips under the command line?
4. On the marshal's card, some order buttons have a dashed edge. What does that mean?
5. On the Congress page, what is a "titled province"?

**The key** — a reader's answer is right when it says this, in any words:
1. Actions are the turn's pool for orders. Giving a marshal an order spends them, 1 or 2 each, and they come
   back when the turn ends. ("Each turn you have a few; an order uses them.")
2. It costs 2, and you have 4 — read from "2 actions of the 4 in hand".
3. It adds those words into the sentence on the line. Nothing is sent until Enter is pressed.
4. That order is not open to him now (locked, unavailable); pointing at it tells why.
5. A province held for good: your homeland, a province a treaty gave you, or a conquest held long enough
   (N turns).

**The bar:** all three readers right on all five questions, each answer "sure" or "fairly sure". This is the bar
row IC's second reading met on its own screens (18 of 18). Any miss is reported as a miss, with the lever that
answers it, and the reading is run again on fresh readers. The row closes only when the bar is met.
