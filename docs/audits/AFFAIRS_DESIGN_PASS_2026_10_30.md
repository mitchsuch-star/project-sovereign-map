### A.2 The rubric — what "good" means

Criteria 5 and 7 are **pass/fail gates**; the rest are scored 1–5 per element with a one-line reason.

1. **A face and a stake.** A named writer in office (office first; a name only where the holder held it through the reachable years, else a voices-by-year table), writing because *they* want something. **Scored on the fallback the client has today** (a monogram, the court's arms, the seal); a portrait is an upgrade behind a §0b question (new public-domain portraits on the user's yes — the S14k precedent, twelve from Commons).
2. **A real fork.** Each answer strictly better than the other on some delivered axis on at least one real board (the nondominance pin); the price printed before the choice (shown = applied, `moments.plan`); the lapse honest and never a purse charge.
3. **History with teeth.** A dated record in the "Historically …" frame; the historically fuller option offered with its real cost, never dropped for being harder (the user's standing lean).
4. **Consequence that shows — net of its tier.** The world visibly answers the choice within a few turns. Reactions are tiered by cost — **T0** a line on an existing seam, **T1** an existing setter, **T2** a new predicate, **T3** a new op — and scored as value net of tier.
5. **GATE — Icing, not load, inside a noise budget.** Never required to win; no new management loop; one open letter; a **noise budget**: at most one reaction line per surface per letter, at most three lines per letter across all surfaces over three turns, one Gazette COURTS line per letter (the column caps at 8), no new rail notice beyond the arrival's own; a **reach band** per template (a premise true on every board is a mandatory letter, not an event).
6. **Alive on real boards.** Fires inside its band on the probed boards (§D) with its premise true; never doubles a surface the spec's §2 maps.
7. **GATE — Buildable, with a claims ledger.** On the moments engine with named seams; every seam, setter and asset claimed carries a file:line or the word *unverified* (an unverified claim caps the score); a pins-flipped list; the ratchet respected (`to_dict` 497 of 500 lines, levers 231, functions over 500 lines 28); no LLM in mechanics; Building Blocks (one function of `(world, lord)`; France's content); Rule 8 (no `world.regions` scan in a tick); **the AI's side stated** — an affair's `relation` or `loyalty` op writes what the moments' premises read (`court_warm`, `vassal_slipping`), so the spec's §7 "invariant by construction" covers pacing, not premises; **save states** stated (empty, unarmed, a pre-RE save); sized in sessions and in **shippable tiers** (the letter alone / + the home / + the reactions).
8. **Discoverable.** The DD-B instrument finds it (a pressable control, never a dispatch row alone); a first-open line (≤ 220 characters, `first_open.py`); every answer a button ("diplomacy is never typed"); above the fold at Interface Scale 1.0 and 2.0 (the 680×440 box is the floor in 800×450 logical at 2.0 — the letter already trimmed a blank line to fit).
9. **Delight (free text).** What would a player retell about this letter?

**(a) Register notes.**
1. *Names.* Napoleon wrote "la colonie", "Santo-Domingo", "les noirs"; Haiti is named only by Haitians. Ferrand's decree (Art. 13) calls them rebels and halts hostilities once they submit to the Emperor of the French. A writer may use the period words; **the record line should say Haiti and Dessalines plainly.**
2. *Decrès* speaks in hulls, crews and sailing windows ("avant l'équinoxe"): "gruff sailor's speech" with Ancien Régime manners (Ramé 1987), "hard, not obliging" (Napoleon, source unnamed); of Napoleon he said he does not mind losing ships if they are lost "avec honneur".
3. *Napoleon* is imperative, numerical, and hates half-measures: "Que rien n'arrête mes escadres"; "ces braves gens"; "une opération d'aucun résultat". On 5 Aug 1805 he regretted "n'avoir pas bien connu les sentiments de cette colonie".
4. *After a loss, an inquiry, not an apology.* 6 Sep 1809: "J'ai ordonné une enquête sur la reddition de Cayenne, du fort Desaix… et de Flessingue"; of Villeneuve, 4 Sep 1805: "un misérable qu'il faut chasser ignominieusement". The *Moniteur* reportedly misstated San Domingo (unconfirmed).
5. *Colons and Haitians.* The colons petition as proprietors and ask for "restauration"; the state answers with "secours". Haitians write as a state: Blanchet to Britain's envoy, Apr 1807 (via Gaffield), "it is rare, Sir, that commercial relations between two peoples do not encourage some kind of political relationship"; Pétion, 28 Dec 1808, "You must be sure of the interest I have in the success of your operations against the French".

**(b) The three most recurring and most forkable.**
1. *The sailing list (row 2).* Seven dated returns; each fork is real (a true squadron, a half-measure, or nothing), each priced in ships, readiness and gold, each outcome on record, so the historically fuller option keeps its teeth. Napoleon's 26 Sep 1809 rule — a real expedition or nothing — is the spine. Writers: Decrès, a captain-general.
2. *Ferrand's garrison (row 1).* The best documented, with a face (Ferrand, then Barquier), dated orders (Aug 1805, Jan 1806, Dec 1808) and Haitian agency. The stake can be the flag and the garrison, never plantation yield; the letter must name the decree of 6 Jan 1805.
3. *The colons' memorial (row 3).* Yearly, with an honest fork (relief from the Emperor's purse, or refusal) and a marked future fact (1825). Runner-up: the Washington ban (the Act of 28 Feb 1806, evaded; Napoleon's 5 Aug 1805 and 2 Sep 1806 naval orders). **For the free slot T35–41, 17 Feb – 25 Mar 1807 offers Christophe's Act, the US renewal (24 Feb), Pétion elected (9 Mar), Britain's abolition (25 Mar).**

**(c) The content stance, applied.** Read the colons' petitions as property claims by a divided group, not as the government's motive (Napoleon later blamed the planters' "criailleries"; historians stress his own aims). Their claim was to a colony whose value, in Pétion's own 1814 reckoning, was one-third people held as slaves. A letter can carry it honestly if the colons ask for relief (what the state gave) and no answer offers restoration. The record line should carry: abolition 4 Feb 1794; the law of 20 May 1802 maintaining slavery and authorising the trade, and the consular acts restoring it in Guadeloupe and Guiana; the expedition (sailed 14 Dec 1801) aimed to destroy Toussaint's government and ended in independence on 1 Jan 1804; the killings of the remaining French in 1804; Ferrand's decree of 6 Jan 1805; Pétion's offer as a marked future fact. **Price relief in gold and relief squadrons in ships, never "yield".**
