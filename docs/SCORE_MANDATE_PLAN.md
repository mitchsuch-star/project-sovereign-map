## §0 The mandate

1. **Every pillar rises; none falls.** Each chunk names the pillar it exists to raise, the rows it lands, and the arm that re-scores it. A chunk is not landed until its re-score line is written into the scoreboard (§1) with its date and its evidence.
2. **Weakest first.** The order of §2 is the order of the scoreboard's gaps: the ending (5.5), diplomacy (6.0), then the four 6.5s (first contact, economy, naval, living balance), then the 7.0s, then the 7.5s. A pillar already at target is served last (that is why most of the CRT queue moved behind the weak pillars — command holds 7.5).
3. **Chunks by category.** One pillar family per chunk. Inside a chunk the owned rows land in the order written; **30% of every chunk's session budget is a reserve for quick wins** drawn from the standing bank (§3), from ANY pillar, chosen at the chunk's start by size and score effect. A chunk that runs long drops its LAST owned row to the next chunk of its category; it never spends the reserve on owned rows.
4. **Player reports jump the queue** (kept from September 23). A row filed from an outside player's report, or from a played campaign's P1, lands ahead of the chunk in progress if it is under half a session; otherwise it heads the next chunk of its category.
5. **Design before build for §4.** Nothing under §4 (technology; asymmetry and national flavor; the AP and DP gaps) is built before its gate returns. Chunk 1 may *measure* against the AP/DP levers (it must, to know whether the road reaches 45); it may not move them.
6. **The four-file rule stands, plus one:** STATUS ▶ NEXT UP, the landing record, the rows disposed, `CLAUDE.md` LIVE STATE — and the scoreboard row in this file.
7. **Balance is attributed, never assumed.** `BASELINE_SERIES` + M1–M7 byte-identical unless a flip experiment attributes the move (the VP-R1 idiom); a chunk that moves the series without an arm is not landed.

## §4 NEEDS DESIGN — after the chunks (three gates; nothing built before they return)

Each gate is a session of research and a memo with the questions answered at recommended defaults, in the project's idiom (`AI_INTENT_SPEC.md` §6 / `HEALTH_CHECK_DESIGN_GATE_2026_08_14.md`). The recommended slot for each is named; the user's ruling places it. **Rule of the house for all three: authored content, not formulas** (`AI_INTENT_SPEC.md` §6 D7 — every varying value carries an authored range in `europe_1805.json`, and the AI takes the same rules by GR5, which is not the same as taking them competently — the seasons memo's lesson).

### SR-D1 Technology — "Reforms, not research"

- **What exists.** Nothing mechanical. ROADMAP Phase 8.5 lists "Light Tech/Reforms — simple upgrades: conscription, tactics, administration — Planned". The economy has the levy, the substitute market, training grounds, the Continental System decree machinery, the marshal's `administration` skill; the naval block has keels; the Cabinet has instruments. There is no research, no tree, and no period justification for one in a 40-turn 1805 campaign.
- **The question.** Should the reign have a handful of STATE REFORMS — bought once, with a currency the game already prices (DP, authority, gold, an admin action), each a named 1805-period act with a stated effect — rather than a technology tree?
- **Candidate shapes.** (i) *Reforms* (recommended): five to eight authored acts per nation with a price and a term — e.g. for France the *Corps System / the Staff* (+1 action point: the AP gap's natural lever, see SR-D3), the *Conscription Law* (the substitute market's size and price), the *Berlin Decree* (Continental System closure as a decree, not only as blockade), the *Artillery Reserve* (the artillery pool and Marmont's arm), the *Imperial Guard expansion*, the *Code* in a client (loyalty); for Prussia the *Army Reform* after a defeat (Scharnhorst — drill and the levy), for Austria the *Landwehr*, for Russia the *Serf Levy* (cheap men at low morale) — each nation's list authored in the scenario. (ii) *Tech tree*: rejected on period grounds unless the gate says otherwise. (iii) *Events* (Phase 8.5's Historical Moments) as the delivery vehicle for reforms — a reform offered as a choice at a beat.
- **Gate questions.** Q1 reforms or nothing (a tree is not on the table); Q2 the currency (DP vs authority vs gold vs an admin action — the recommendation is authority for the political acts and gold for the material ones); Q3 the count and the term (5–8 per major, permanent vs 12-turn); Q4 both boards (GR5) — and how the AI chooses (a deck order, like the agendas); Q5 whether the Staff reform IS the AP lever (SR-D3) or a separate thing; Q6 interplay with seasons (HC-6) — the Russian reforms and the General Winter are one design conversation.
- **What not to build first:** anything that changes a boot number (every reform is off at boot; `BASELINE_SERIES` byte-identical by construction).
- **Slot:** after Chunk 5 (economy) — a reform is an economy and command lever and the gate wants those pillars' numbers settled first.

### SR-D2 Asymmetry and national flavor — "France feels different from Austria"

- **What exists.** The courts already differ by authored numbers and rosters: statecraft profiles (Austria the coalition-builder, Prussia the bandwagoner, Russia the arbiter, Britain the paymaster — `AI_INTENT_SPEC.md` §3.4, scored 8.0 for "distinct statesmen"), agenda decks, the navies block, capital garrison tiers, marshal skills and abilities, the letter-book's voices (DEF-1's fallbacks for eleven courts), the paymaster's purse, the Continental System. What does NOT differ: the mechanics. Every nation recruits, marches, musters, starves and fortifies by one rulebook with authored constants; the player is always France; "National Flavor — Planned" and "Multi-Nation Play — post-EA" are the ROADMAP's only words on it.
- **The question.** Which THREE asymmetries would make the 1805 board read as five different armies rather than one army in five coats — without a second playable nation?
- **Candidate shapes.** (i) *National doctrines* as authored modifiers with a sentence: France's corps system (the muster's arrival odds and the SUPPORT lever are the mechanic — a doctrine names and tunes them), Britain's sea and subsidy (exists) plus a land army that cannot be replaced (the 5,000-man raids are that), Russia's depth (serf levies at low morale, the General Winter — HC-6 — and a retreat that does not break), Austria's archdukes (the quarrel exists; the Landwehr reform is SR-D1's), Prussia's drill (a drilled corps's bonus larger, its fragility after Jena — the emergent Revanche exists). (ii) *Historical Moments* (Phase 8.5): the Coronation, Tilsit, the Retreat — authored beats with a choice, the same machinery as the Proclamation. (iii) *Multi-nation play*: out of scope for this gate (post-EA), but every doctrine must be written so a Britain player could inherit it.
- **Gate questions.** Q1 which three first (recommendation: Russia's depth + winter, Britain's irreplaceable army, France's staff/corps doctrine); Q2 modifiers vs mechanics (a modifier is a number the AI already plays; a mechanic needs an AI rung — the seasons memo's "symmetric rules are not symmetric competence"); Q3 where they are authored (the scenario's `statecraft`/`nation_config` blocks, validator-clamped); Q4 whether Historical Moments ship with the doctrines or after; Q5 the measurement (the AI-V sweep's homogeneity guard + a played arm that can NAME each court's style).
- **What not to build first:** a second playable nation; anything the AI cannot play.
- **Slot:** after Chunk 7 (AI aliveness) — the doctrines are AI behaviour as much as player flavor, and HC-6 (seasons, deferred past Round 0) belongs in the same ruling.

### SR-D3 The AP and DP gaps — "four action points for eight corps"

- **What exists.** Four military actions and two administrative ones a turn, fixed (`world_state.max_actions` / `max_admin_actions`); a strategic order costs 2 (1 for a literal marshal and for the sovereign); unused admin actions pay 25 gold each; the turn auto-ends when both pools are spent (AAR-12). Diplomatic points: five a turn (base 3 + Talleyrand's skill + authority), the Seat +1 with the Emperor at Paris; a courtship 2 a turn, a client's petition 1, an armistice 1, a separate peace 3, a summons 2. The AI's marshals move themselves (every corps acts every turn); the player's do not.
- **The measured gaps.** §68.6: half the army idles every turn; the hand-played arms used all four and still could not both strike and cover; the AI's corps all move. AAR-D5: one client's petition (1 DP) cost the Austrian peace a turn (the chip greyed for want of a third point); the Seat's +1 needs an Emperor who is never at Paris in a campaign. The standing orders (march / hold / support) are the intended multiplier and the two played reviews used them little because each costs 2 and the tail is fragile (AAR-9, AAR-10).
- **Candidate shapes.** (a) *AP scales with the roster*: one action per two corps, floor 4, ceiling 6 — the simplest, and the one the reach measurement most wants; (b) *the Staff reform* (SR-D1) buys the fifth and sixth point — the same number, earned; (c) *standing orders cheaper*: SUPPORT at 1 AP, a march that keeps its tail through a reinforcement (SR-4b) — the multiplier the design already intends, made reliable before any new points are minted; (d) *DP banking*: unspent points carry one turn (cap 7), or the separate peace priced at 2, or the war room names the DP price of the road it recommends (SR-1c takes the last of these regardless); (e) *admin actions convertible*: an unused admin action becomes a diplomatic point instead of 25 gold.
- **Gate questions.** Q1 whether AP should move at all before Chunk 1's re-measure says the road needs it (recommendation: (c) first — it is legibility, not balance — then read SR-1e); Q2 (a) vs (b) — a free scaling or an earned reform; Q3 DP: bank, reprice, or counsel only; Q4 the AI's parity (the AI already acts with every corps; a player AP rise is a player-only change and the series will move — attribute it); Q5 the admin pool (convert, or leave).
- **What not to build first:** a fifth action point without the flip attribution (the whole reach measurement depends on it); anything that changes what a literal marshal pays.
- **Slot:** immediately after Chunk 1 IF SR-1e says the road stops short (§2, Chunk 1); otherwise after Chunk 4 (the standing-order pass is (c)'s prerequisite).
